# Threads of Greatness — Endorsement / Blurb Kit

A launch-title from a first-time KDP author lives or dies on trust signals. Two or three
strong endorsements do more for conversion than almost any copy edit. This kit gives you
the where, the who, and the exact outreach language.

---

## 1. Where endorsements go (in priority order)

1. **Back cover** — 1 to 3 short blurbs (1–2 sentences each). The single highest-value spot.
2. **"Praise for Threads of Greatness" page** — the very first page of the front matter,
   before the title page. This is where you stack the rest (4–8 blurbs), longest first.
3. **Amazon "Editorial Reviews" section** — paste the same blurbs into the KDP listing's
   Editorial Reviews field (separate from the book description). Free, and most authors skip it.
4. **A+ Content module** — one standout blurb, set large, as a graphic.

A blurb can appear in all four places at once. Collect more than you need; you curate later.

---

## 2. Who to ask (target categories, not a fixed list)

Aim for a spread across the book's three audiences so the praise page signals "this crosses over."

- **Christian education / homeschool voices** — authors, conference speakers, co-op leaders,
  Charlotte Mason / classical-education figures. (Your Sources page already cites this world —
  Mason, Tripp, Smith — which is a natural warm-lead map.)
- **Leadership / talent-development voices** — executives, exec coaches, org-development
  consultants, HR leaders. These validate the Workplace Parallel spine for the professional reader.
- **Ministry / apostolic-network leaders** — pastors, ministry heads, and leaders from the
  130-nation Awakening Destiny Global network. Your warmest, fastest yeses; lead here.
- **Educators with a platform** — a respected principal, superintendent, or professor of
  education who will lend institutional credibility.

Sourcing order: (1) your own ADG network and past cohort leaders, (2) people you've served or
spoken alongside, (3) authors whose work you cite — a genuine "your work shaped this chapter"
note earns a surprising number of replies. Do not buy blurbs or trade for reciprocity; readers
can smell it, and it puts your name at risk.

Target ~12 asks to land 4–6 usable blurbs. Endorsers say yes, then go silent; over-ask.

---

## 3. The outreach email (copy/paste, then personalize the first line)

> **Subject:** A short ask — would you endorse *Threads of Greatness*?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> [One specific, genuine sentence — e.g., "Your work on X has shaped how Donna and I
> think about formation for years," or "Ever since [shared moment], I've wanted your eyes
> on this."]
>
> Donna and I have finished a book called *Threads of Greatness: Educating Children,
> Developing Talent, and Forming Lifelong Learners*. It lays out nine practical
> "enhancements" for forming whole people — and each chapter carries a workplace parallel,
> so it speaks to leaders and teams as much as to parents and teachers.
>
> Would you be open to writing a short endorsement (1–3 sentences is perfect)? I'd be glad
> to send the full manuscript or just the chapters closest to your world — whatever is
> easiest. If it helps, I can also draft a line or two in your voice for you to edit or
> discard entirely.
>
> To make the print deadline, I'd need it by **[date — give ~3 weeks]**. No pressure at all
> if the timing doesn't work.
>
> With gratitude,
> Will (and Donna) Meier
> Awakening Destiny Global

**Why it works:** it makes the ask tiny (1–3 sentences), removes friction (offers the excerpt
*and* a ghost-drafted option), and names a real deadline. The "I can draft a line for you"
offer roughly doubles yes-rates from busy people — many *want* to help but won't find the time
to write from scratch.

---

## 4. Timeline (work backward from your publish date)

- **6 weeks out:** send all ~12 asks. Attach the manuscript or a clean excerpt PDF.
- **3 weeks out:** soft reminder to non-responders ("totally fine to pass — just closing the list").
- **2 weeks out:** finalize the praise page and back-cover selections; paste into Editorial Reviews.
- **Launch:** the praise page is set; keep collecting — later blurbs still feed Editorial Reviews
  and A+ Content even after publication.

---

## 5. Format each blurb consistently

> "One or two vivid sentences that say something specific, not generic praise."
> — **First Last**, *Title / Role*, Organization; author of *Book* (if relevant)

Prefer specific over superlative: "The chapter on adversity reframed how I coach my own team"
beats "A must-read for every parent." Specific praise is more credible and more quotable.
